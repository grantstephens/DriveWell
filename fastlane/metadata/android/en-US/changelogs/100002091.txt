Rebuilt how jerk is scored, from scratch, on a proper foundation this time.

Two more real drives — a highway and a rough country road — showed beta.8's
approach was still wrong: real harsh braking and acceleration weren't
reliably showing up, the live number bounced around too much, and an
event's effect on the score faded almost as fast as it appeared. Rather
than tune more constants, this build replaces the whole mechanism with a
sliding-window comparison: how rough the last couple of seconds have been
versus how rough the last 30 seconds have been. That's a standard signal-
processing pattern (used for detecting transients in audio and seismic
data), and it's structurally close to ISO 2631, the actual engineering
standard for vibration comfort — not something invented for this app.

A real window, unlike the exponential-average approach the last two builds
used, naturally remembers a harsh moment for several seconds after it
happens and naturally smooths out noise, without needing extra tuning on
top. Checked against all four real drives captured so far: the two newest
went from 30-46 separate, sub-second score dips each, to 2-9 dips lasting
several seconds apiece — which is what "the leaf actually reacts to what
just happened, and remembers it for a bit" should look like.

Also fixes a real bug found while building this: a stop longer than 30
seconds could silently erase what the engine had learned about the current
road, the same category of bug from a few betas back, now fixed the same
way at a different layer.

Heads up: scores will look different again from beta.8, and that's the
point — beta.8 was still quietly under-reacting to real events on some
roads. If a drive feels wrong either direction, a fresh "Export last drive"
is still the fastest way to get it looked at.

Still a beta: validated against four real drives, not a fleet.
