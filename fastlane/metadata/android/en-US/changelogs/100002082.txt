Redesigned how jerk is scored — real harsh events should actually register now.

A follow-up debug export (this time captured with beta.7's fix, so genuinely
sampled at ~50 Hz) showed the same complaint persisting: real, sharp braking
and acceleration events barely dented the score. The cause this time wasn't
sampling, it was the scoring model itself — jerk was judged against a single
hand-picked absolute threshold, fitted to whatever one earlier drive's
ambient road noise happened to look like, with no reason to generalize to a
rougher or smoother road.

Jerk is now judged against a rolling, self-learned "normal for this drive"
— how many typical deviations above it the current reading is, not a fixed
number. This adapts to the actual road instead of needing to be retuned
every time someone reports it feels off. Checked against two real drives
with very different ambient noise levels: same approach worked for both
without touching a constant, and events that previously barely dropped
the score (94-99%) now read as 0-47%, in proportion to how rough they
actually were.

Also fixes a bug found while chasing this down: the "baseline survives a
stop" protection from a couple of betas back was quietly leaking — for
about 30 seconds after a real stop, it kept adjusting to the dying-down
signal before finally freezing, eroding most of what it had learned. Fixed
at the root.

Heads up: trip scores will read noticeably lower than beta.7 for the same
driving — roughly 5 points lower on both drives used to validate this. Not
a regression: the score is now honestly reflecting events beta.7 (and
everything before it) was quietly hiding.

Still a beta: validated against two real drives, not a fleet. If a genuinely
smooth drive scores lower than it should, or a rough one still reads too
high, a fresh "Export last drive" is the fastest way to get it looked at.
