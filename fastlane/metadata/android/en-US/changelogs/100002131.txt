DriveWell is now Featherfoot — plus pausing, and a leaf that tells you when it's going wrong.

Renamed: the old name belonged to an existing driving-telematics product, so
the app is now Featherfoot (an old idiom for a gentle foot on the pedals —
the opposite of a lead foot). Because the app's identity changed, Android
treats this as a separate app: it installs alongside your old DriveWell,
starts with an empty history, and doesn't carry your old trips over. Once
you're happy with it you can uninstall the old one. Everything else — the
smoothness score, the end-of-drive traffic lights, the share card — is the
same, just with the new name on it.

New: pauses itself when it isn't on screen. Android stops giving sensor data
to an app that isn't in front, which used to leave a drive quietly blind
whenever you switched to a navigation app (one real drive lost nine of
seventeen minutes that way). Now Featherfoot pauses when you leave it and
picks up where it left off when you come back. Time it couldn't see isn't
scored or counted as driving time, and the end-of-drive card tells you how
much there was.

New: the leaf now shades from green through amber to red instead of green to
brown, to match the traffic lights. Because red and green look alike to a lot
of drivers, colour is never the only warning: the leaf wilts when things get
rough, the edges of the screen glow amber and then red where you can see them
without looking, a red glow breathes slowly, a single ripple marks a harsh
moment, and a soft bloom marks the recovery. None of it moves while you're
driving well, nothing flashes, and it all holds still if your phone's
reduce-motion setting is on.

Still a beta: the effects and the braking/acceleration lights are checked
against synthetic and a handful of real drives, not a fleet. If anything
looks or feels wrong, "Export last drive" in Settings is the fastest way to
get it looked at.
